# 🛍️ Customer Shopping Behavior Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project that explores customer shopping behavior using **Python (Pandas)** for data cleaning, **PostgreSQL** for querying, and **Power BI** for interactive dashboarding.

The project follows a complete analytics pipeline: raw CSV data → cleaned & transformed dataset (Python) → relational database (PostgreSQL) → business insights (SQL) → interactive visual reporting (Power BI).

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Retailers generate huge volumes of transactional data, but raw data rarely tells a story on its own. This project takes a raw customer shopping dataset of **3,900 transactions** and turns it into actionable insights — covering revenue trends, customer demographics, product performance, subscription behavior, and discount effectiveness.

**Key questions answered:**
- Who are our customers, and how does spending differ by gender and age group?
- Which products and categories drive the most revenue and the highest ratings?
- Do subscribers spend more than non-subscribers?
- Does applying a discount actually correlate with higher spending?
- Which shipping methods and payment methods are most common?
- How does customer behavior vary by U.S. state?

## 🧹 Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering (Python)

Performed in `Customer_Shopping_Behavior_Analysis.ipynb`:

1. **Loaded** the raw CSV with Pandas and inspected it using `.head()`, `.info()`, and `.describe(include='all')`.
2. **Handled missing values** — `Review Rating` had 37 nulls, imputed using the **median rating per product category** (`groupby('Category').transform`) rather than a single dataset-wide median, to preserve category-level rating patterns.
3. **Standardized column names** to `snake_case` for consistency and easier SQL querying (e.g. `Purchase Amount (USD)` → `purchase_amount`).
4. **Feature engineering:**
   - `age_group` — binned `age` into `Young`, `Adult`, `Middle Aged`, and `Senior Citizen` using `pd.cut()`.
   - `purchase_frequency_days` — mapped categorical purchase frequency labels (e.g. `Weekly`, `Monthly`, `Annually`) to a numeric day count for trend analysis.
5. **Redundancy check** — discovered `discount_applied` and `promo_code_used` were **100% identical columns** (verified via `.all()` comparison) and dropped the duplicate (`promo_code_used`) to keep the dataset lean.
6. **Loaded the cleaned DataFrame into PostgreSQL** using `SQLAlchemy` + `psycopg2`, writing it to a `customer` table inside the `customer_behavior` database for downstream SQL analysis.

**Final cleaned schema (19 columns):** `customer_id`, `age`, `gender`, `item_purchased`, `category`, `purchase_amount`, `location`, `size`, `color`, `season`, `review_rating`, `subscription_status`, `shipping_type`, `discount_applied`, `previous_purchases`, `payment_method`, `frequency_of_purchases`, `age_group`, `purchase_frequency_days`.

## 🗄️ SQL Analysis (PostgreSQL)

Business questions answered directly in the database using aggregate functions, `GROUP BY`, subqueries, and `CASE` statements:

| # | Business Question | Technique Used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Total revenue generated by male vs. female customers | `SUM()` + `GROUP BY gender` |
| 2 | Which customers used a discount but still spent above the average purchase amount? | Correlated subquery (`>= (SELECT AVG(...))`) |
| 3 | Top 5 products by highest average review rating | `GROUP BY` + `ORDER BY AVG(...) DESC` + `LIMIT` |
| 4 | Compare average purchase amount: Standard vs. Express shipping | `WHERE ... IN (...)` + `GROUP BY` |
| 5 | Do subscribed customers spend more than non-subscribers? | `GROUP BY subscription_status` with total & average revenue |
| 6 | Top 5 products with the highest % of purchases using a discount | `CASE WHEN` + `SUM()` for a calculated discount rate |

Full query set available in [`sql/customer_behavior_queries.sql`](sql/customer_behavior.sql).

## 📊 Power BI Dashboard

A 4-page interactive Power BI report built on top of the cleaned dataset, with cross-page filters for **season, size, category, gender, age group, item, color, and payment method**.

### 1️⃣ Overview
High-level KPIs — total customers, total revenue, average rating, average purchase amount, total orders, and total items — plus a **U.S. map** showing revenue/customer distribution by state and a location filter panel.

### 2️⃣ Customer Insights
- Customer & revenue split by **gender** (68% male in this dataset)
- Item count by **category**
- **Subscribers vs. non-subscribers** by age group (trend area chart)
- Revenue breakdown by **age and gender** (clustered bar chart)

### 3️⃣ Product Performance
- Revenue and item count by **category**
- Average rating distribution (donut chart)
- **Top 10 products** ranked by count sold, total revenue, and average rating (matrix table)
- Combined bar + line chart of **total revenue vs. average rating** per item

### 4️⃣ Payments & Discounts
- Revenue by **payment method** (Credit Card, PayPal, Cash, Debit Card, Venmo, Bank Transfer)
- Customer distribution by payment method (donut chart)
- **Discount claim rate** (57% of customers used a discount)
- Combined view of revenue and discount-customer count by item purchased

**Data model** (Power BI): a single `Customer` fact table with calculated measures including `Total Revenue`, `Total Customers`, `Total Transactions`, `Average Rating`, `Subscription Rate`, `Discount Rate`, `Avg Purchase Frequency (Days)`, and `Target Subscription Rate`.

## 🔑 Key Insights

- 📦 **3,900 transactions**, **~$233K total revenue**, average purchase amount of **$59.76**, average customer rating of **3.75/5**.
- 👥 The customer base skews heavily male (**68%**), and male customers drive over **2x** the revenue of female customers (**$158K vs. $75K**).
- 🎽 **Clothing** is the dominant category by items sold, followed by Accessories, Footwear, and Outerwear.
- 🔁 Only **~27%** of customers are subscribers, yet subscribers show meaningfully different purchase behavior than non-subscribers across age groups.
- 🏷️ **57%** of customers have used a discount, but discount usage doesn't strongly correlate with higher-than-average spend for most items.
- 💳 Payment methods are fairly evenly distributed (all five options cluster around 15–17% share each), suggesting no dominant preferred payment channel.
- ⭐ Product ratings are tightly clustered between **3.6 and 3.9**, indicating consistent customer satisfaction with no major outlier products.

## ▶️ How to Reproduce

1. **Clone the repo** and set up a Python environment:
   ```bash
   pip install pandas sqlalchemy psycopg2-binary
   ```
2. **Run the notebook** (`notebooks/Customer_Shopping_Behavior_Analysis.ipynb`) to clean the raw CSV and load it into PostgreSQL. Update the connection credentials (`username`, `password`, `host`, `port`, `database`) to match your local setup.
3. **Run the SQL queries** in `sql/customer_behavior_queries.sql` using pgAdmin or any PostgreSQL client to reproduce the analytical results.
4. **Open the dashboard** — launch `dashboard/Customer_Shopping_Behavior.pbix` in Power BI Desktop and refresh the data source to point to your PostgreSQL instance (or the CSV directly).
